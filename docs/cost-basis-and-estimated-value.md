# Cost Basis And Estimated Value

FlipRadar separates what a collector paid from what the market currently suggests
an item may be worth. This keeps historical purchase decisions distinct from a
market estimate that can change whenever new pricing data is collected.

## Cost Basis

Cost basis is the recorded purchase price multiplied by quantity. It is shown in
the currency supplied when a portfolio item is added. It does not include selling
fees, taxes, storage, restoration, or shipping unless those costs were included
in the price entered by the collector.

For example, two sealed sets recorded at $285 each have a $570 cost basis. Editing
the purchase price or quantity changes cost basis; a market refresh does not.

## Estimated Value

Estimated value is the latest available fair-market-value snapshot appropriate to
the holding's condition, multiplied by its quantity. New and sealed holdings use
new-market data; used holdings prefer used-complete data. When no suitable
snapshot exists, the holding is marked as unvalued rather than inventing a value.

The figure is an estimate, not a guaranteed sale price. It does not subtract
marketplace fees, fulfillment costs, or the effect of a particular listing's
condition, location, bundle contents, or seller reputation.

## Gain Or Loss

Unrealized gain or loss is estimated value minus cost basis. It is *unrealized*
because a sale has not occurred. The percentage is calculated from cost basis,
so it is unavailable when a holding has no meaningful recorded cost basis.

Use these figures to compare holdings and identify items to research further—not
as tax reporting, financial advice, or a prediction of a sale outcome. For the
strength and age of the underlying market data, see
[Confidence, Freshness, and Data Limits](confidence-freshness-and-data-limits.md).
